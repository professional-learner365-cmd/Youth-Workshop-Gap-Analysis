# Youth Workshop Gap Analysis

## Using Public Data to Identify Youth Program Gaps and Recommend Practical Workshop Opportunities
This project analyzes public program, education, demographic, and volunteering data to identify gaps in youth programming and translate those findings into practical recommendations.

### Tools and Skills Used

**Excel | PivotTables | Data Cleaning | Exploratory Data Analysis | Gap Analysis | Data Visualization | Decision Support**

---

# Key Findings
- Sports and arts were the most common youth workshop topics.
- Life Skills, Literacy, Public Speaking and Volunteering appeared less covered by existing offerings.
- The most underserved age group is: 18-24 and the youth/adult age groups.
- Employment/Life Skills, Leadership, Public Speaking and Online were identified as areas to prioritize first.

# Gaps
- The number and availability of workshops across categories, age groups and locations varied based on the datasets.

## City_of_Toronto_Courses_15/16
- Few programs for a certain age group
  - Child: Least amount of programs for Volunteering, Public Speaking, Literacy, Leadership, Life Skills, STEM.
  - Child/Youth: Public Speaking, Leadership. Literacy, Leadership, STEM
  - Youth: Public Speaking, Leadership. Literacy, STEM, Volunteering
  - Youth/Adult: Public Speaking, Literacy, STEM, Volunteering, Life Skills
  - Adult: Public Speaking, Literacy, Life Skills, Leadership, STEM, Volunteering, Life Skills
- Topics with limited availability: Employment, Life Skills, Leadership, Literacy, STEM, Volunteering, Public Speaking
- Programs concentrated in only a few cities: Courses related to literacy, life skills, leadership and volunteerism aren’t as common as sports and arts courses.
- Lack of free or low-cost options: doesn’t mention cost, however City of Toronto courses may either be free or paid courses.
- Limited online or accessible delivery: doesn’t mention method of delivery

  - 161 volunteer-related programs

# Workshop Ideas

## Which workshop ideas should be prioritized first?
- Employment/Life Skills, Leadership, Public Speaking, Online

## What new workshops should be considered developing?

### 1. Employment Tips: Public Speaking Skills for Interviews
- Target audience: 18-24 / 18+
- Main learning outcomes:
  - Participants feel more equipped/ prepared for interviews for employment
  - Participants feel more confident speaking in public settings
- Reason it is needed: More employment-related programs and programs for youth/adults and 18+ persons are needed
- Suggested delivery format: Online
- Basic success measures: Attendance, Feedback, Expression of Interest in other programs

### 2. Employment Tips: Practical Leadership Tips
- Target audience: 18-24 / 18+
- Main learning outcomes:
  - Participants feel more prepared for leadership roles
  - Participants feel confident working in leadership roles
- Reason it is needed: More programs are needed for youth/adults and 18+ persons are needed
- Suggested delivery format: Online
- Basic success measures: Attendance, Feedback, Expression of Interest in other programs

### 3. Youth Life Skills Tips
- Target audience: 18-24 / 18+
- Main learning outcomes:
  - Participants feel more prepared for different opportunities (post-secondary education, trades, employment, cooking, baking, etc.)
- Reason it is needed: More programs are needed for youth/adults and 18+ persons are needed
- Suggested delivery format: Online
- Basic success measures: Attendance, Feedback, Expression of Interest in other programs

- Target audience: 18-24 / 18+

# Limitations
- Census data for persons aged 12-14 only was not available, only 10 to 14 was present. Therefore, we don’t know the specific population size for this group.
- TPL_Events_26/27 dataset did not have an age group that included the age 12 within the target ages 12-24, therefore the 6-12 age group was included in the analysis.
- Datasets did not include the costs, however TPL events are usually free and the cost of City of Toronto courses would vary based on the type.
- Some datasets may not accurately reflect the current course types available, e.g. City_of_Toronto_Courses_15/16 dataset.
- Age groups are not clearly defined in numbers for accurate comparison among all datasets, e.g City_of_Toronto_Courses_15/16 data set uses word labels for ages - ‘youth’, ‘youth/adult’, etc.
- Criteria used to determine High, Medium and Low Opportunity Areas in City_of_Toronto_Courses_15/16 and TPL_Events_26/27 datasets may need to be adjusted. Especially since in the City_of_Toronto_Courses_15/16 dataset all courses were identified as being high opportunity areas. However, this may also be a signal for courses to be more diverse for multiple age groups rather than target only certain ages.
